Workflows are scenarios where the AI model has a single, well-defined task. The AI Toolkit includes these built-in workflows:

<Cards>
  <Card title="Insert content" href="/docs/ai/ai-toolkit/client/workflows/insert-content">
    Insert content in a single place of the document.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Proofreader" href="/docs/ai/ai-toolkit/client/workflows/proofreader">
    Correct mistakes like spelling and grammar.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tiptap Edit" href="/docs/ai/ai-toolkit/client/workflows/tiptap-edit">
    A general-purpose workflow for making small and large edits to Tiptap documents.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Comments" href="/docs/ai/ai-toolkit/client/workflows/comments">
    Manage comments and threads in your Tiptap documents.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Template" href="/docs/ai/ai-toolkit/client/workflows/template">
    Fill structured templates with AI-generated content.
  </Card>
</Cards>

Each built-in workflow includes

1. A ready-to-use prompt for the AI model.
2. A method to execute the workflow and apply the edits to the Tiptap Editor.

Built-in workflows can be configured and extended. Additionally, you can create your own workflows from scratch with the AI Toolkit methods.

## Workflows vs. AI Agents

In [AI agents](/docs/ai/ai-toolkit/client/agents), the AI model can choose from a set of tools to perform a task. Workflows, on the other hand, only allow the AI model to perform a single action, such as generating edits or rewriting the document.

In general, workflows are simpler and faster to implement than AI agent tools. However, AI agents are more flexible and can be used to perform more complex tasks.
