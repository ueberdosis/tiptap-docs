## Why Composable Documents

Most products built around documents make a trade-off. Either they split content into forms, fields, and settings screens, which are easy to validate but break up the writing. Or they put everything into one rich text area, which keeps people in context but makes it hard to find, reuse, validate, or protect parts of the content.

Composable Documents keep one readable document and give your application the structure behind it. The document knows what belongs where, which values come from your application, and who may change or read each part. Start with the part your workflow needs and add the others later.

## The parts

### Slots

Slots are named places in a document with their own rules. A Slot can carry a label, instructions for the person filling it, and rules for what a valid value is, such as whether it is required or which content it allows. A headline, a contract exception, or an answer in an application stays part of the text, while your application can find, fill, and validate it.

[Slots overview](/composable-docs/slots/getting-started/overview)

### Variables

Variables connect a document to values your application manages, such as a customer name, an author, or a calculated total. The document stores a reference, not a copy of the value, so every place that shows it updates when the value changes. Variables work in ordinary text and inside Slots.

[Variables overview](/composable-docs/variables/getting-started/overview)

### Content Protection

Content Protection sets what users may change or read. A policy selects Nodes, Marks, attributes, or content and allows or denies editing and reading. With it, you can:

- keep passages from being changed or removed,
- allow only some changes inside a passage, such as text edits but not a new heading level,
- hide content from users who may not read it, behind a placeholder you can style.

You can change the policy at runtime, and in a collaborative document each person can have their own.

[Content Protection overview](/composable-docs/content-protection/getting-started/overview)

## How they work together

- **A form that can only be filled.** Content Protection keeps everything outside the Slots fixed and assigns Slots by role. [Allow filling only](/composable-docs/slots/guides/fill-only)
- **One value in several places.** Bind Slots to a Variable, so a value and every field that shows it stay the same. [Bind slots to variables](/composable-docs/variables/guides/bind-slots)
- **Fields in a table.** Fill table cells as Slots while headers and columns stay fixed. [Table fields example](/composable-docs/slots/examples/table-fields)
- **Shared rules, different permissions.** People edit the same document together. Each has their own policy, while the document and its Slot rules stay the same for everyone. [Collaboration](/composable-docs/content-protection/getting-started/overview#collaboration)

## Use cases

| Use case                     | What it uses                                                                            | Parts                                |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Grant applications and forms | A document that is only its fields, with instructions, limits, and validation.          | Slots, Content Protection            |
| Contracts and proposals      | Approved clauses that stay fixed, marked places for exceptions, and customer values.    | Slots, Variables, Content Protection |
| Newsletters and letters      | Names and campaign data from your application, shown per recipient.                     | Variables                            |
| Support replies              | Fields inside ordinary text, each with its own instructions and limits.                 | Slots                                |
| Clinical notes and records   | Passages that can't be changed, and personal data hidden from those who may not see it. | Content Protection                   |
| Regulated records            | Fields, a specification table with fixed columns, and parts only some people sign.      | Slots, Content Protection            |
