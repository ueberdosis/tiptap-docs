## Quick start

Virtualization is on by default. Switch it off to draw every sheet all the time:

```js
PageLayout.configure({
  setup: pageSetup('A4'),
  virtualization: false, // every sheet drawn, all the time
})
```

| Option           | Type              | Default | Description                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------- | ----------------- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `virtualization` | `boolean`         | `true`  | Whether only the sheets near the reader are drawn                                                                                                              |
| `layoutHint`     | `LayoutHintStore` | —       | Where the engine keeps a document's layout between two openings. See [Opening on the same pages](/page-layout/guides/long-documents#opening-on-the-same-pages) |

Switch it off where something outside the editor has to find the text of every sheet in the DOM
at once. See [What only sees the drawn sheets](/page-layout/guides/long-documents#what-only-sees-the-drawn-sheets).

## What stays the same

- **Every sheet is there.** The scrollbar is as long as the document, and a sheet stands where
  it always stood.
- **Every answer covers the whole document.** Page numbers, the page map and `pageAt` answer for
  every page.
- **The document does not change.** What is saved, shared or exported is the same with the
  option on as with it off.

## Which sheets are drawn

- the sheets in view,
- a few ahead of the scroll, more the faster the reader scrolls, and one behind,
- the sheets the caret and the selection start and end on, wherever they are,
- the sheet whose header or footer is being edited.

The other sheets are paper of the right size:

- **Placeholders.** A sheet that is not drawn carries `data-tt-page-placeholder`.
- **Filled as the reader arrives.** A few placeholders are filled per frame, so a fast scroll can
  show an empty sheet for a moment, never a gap.
- **A click fills it.** A click on an empty sheet fills it and puts the caret where the click was.

Which ones they are right now is asked of the engine:

```js
engine.getDrawnPageIndices() // [3, 4, 5, 6]: the pages drawn with their text, in order
```

- **Every page** while `virtualization` is off, and none in [Pageless](/page-layout/features/page-format#pageless)
  mode.
- **It changes as the reader scrolls.** The `onChange` option of `createPageLayoutEditor` is
  called when it does. An overlay or a list of thumbnails reads it there, rather than looking for
  `data-tt-page-placeholder`, which nothing tells it about.

## Selecting

- **Selections work as before.** Selecting everything, moving the caret to the end or setting the
  selection from a command of yours draws the sheets the selection lands on before it is shown.
- **Sheets inside the selection.** A sheet inside the selection that is still paper is filled
  with the selection color and carries `data-tt-page-selected`.
- **The color.** It is `--tt-page-selection-fill`, `Highlight` unless you set it.

## Typing and pasting

- **Typing keeps its pace.** While you type, only the sheets around the caret are laid out
  again. Once you pause, the rest of the document is paginated, and the sheets far from the
  caret are measured in the background.
- **Nothing on screen changes** while the background measuring runs.
- **Headers and footers too.** A line typed into the running head of a book costs what a line in
  the text costs. Sheets that are not on screen take the new head when they are scrolled to.
- **Pasting a long text** puts the caret at the end of what was pasted and scrolls there, as Word
  does.
- **After a paste.** The pages behind the caret are placed from estimates and measured over the
  following seconds. See [Right after opening or pasting](/page-layout/guides/long-documents#right-after-opening-or-pasting).

## Right after opening or pasting

A long document is not measured all at once, so the editor can be used at once rather than after a
long wait. Its pages are placed from estimates first, then measured a slice at a time over the
following seconds.

- **How long.** The 146 pages of the demo above take about a second and a half on a desktop. A
  book of several hundred pages pasted at once can take minutes.
- **Scrolling far away at once.** Sheets you reach before they are measured are measured first,
  and laid out again where an estimate was off. For a moment after you arrive, text on them can
  move to the page before or after, and their page numbers can change.
- **The page count** and the page map settle as the measuring goes on.
- **Knowing when it is done.** `engine.getDiagnostics().measurementPending` is `true` while any
  block is still on its estimate. Read it in `onChange`, which is called as the measuring moves on.
- **Jumping on purpose.** Code that takes the reader far into the document right after loading,
  such as a link to a heading near the end, can wait until `measurementPending` is `false`.
- **Opening without estimates.** A layout hint opens the document on the pages it was closed on.
  See [Opening on the same pages](/page-layout/guides/long-documents#opening-on-the-same-pages).

```js
const engine = editor.storage.pageLayout.engine

if (engine.getDiagnostics().measurementPending) {
  status.textContent = 'Laying out the pages…'
}
```

## Opening on the same pages

A long document opens on estimates, as [Right after opening or pasting](/page-layout/guides/long-documents#right-after-opening-or-pasting)
describes. Give the engine a store, and the document opens on the pages it was closed on instead:

```js
PageLayout.configure({
  setup: pageSetup('A4'),
  layoutHint: {
    load: async (key) => {
      const stored = localStorage.getItem(key)
      return stored === null ? null : JSON.parse(stored)
    },
    save: (key, hint) => localStorage.setItem(key, JSON.stringify(hint)),
  },
})
```

| Method | Arguments   | Returns                       | Called                                                                                                 |
| ------ | ----------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `load` | `key`       | `Promise<LayoutHint \| null>` | Once when the document opens                                                                           |
| `save` | `key, hint` | `void`                        | Once the document is measured and has been still for a second, and after every later edit that settles |

- **Content that arrives later.** If the editor opened on an empty document and the content
  arrived afterwards, `load` is called once more under the key of the document that arrived. The
  first sync of Tiptap Collaboration and a `setContent` after a fetch are both such cases.
- **The key.** It names the document, the paper and the font. A hint is used only for the
  document it was saved from, on the same paper, in a browser that sets text the same way.
- **Anything else is ignored.** A hint under another key is not used, and the document opens on
  estimates.
- **A hint is a starting point.** Every block is still measured, and a hint that turns out wrong
  is corrected the way an estimate is.
- **Nothing of it is in the document.** The same document opens the same with or without a hint.
- **Size.** A hint for a book-length document is about a megabyte of JSON. `save` may drop it, and
  `load` may answer `null`.
- **Keeping hints your own way.** `engine.getLayoutHint()` answers with the current hint, or
  `null` while the document is still being measured.

## Printing

- **The whole document prints.** `window.print()` draws every sheet from `beforeprint` to
  `afterprint`.
- **A viewport of your own.** A surface that tracks its own viewport and reports it with
  `engine.setVisiblePageRange(first, last)` has those sheets drawn at once, whatever else is in
  view.

## What only sees the drawn sheets

- **Find in the browser.** The browser's own find (Cmd+F / Ctrl+F) reads the DOM, so it reaches
  the text of the drawn sheets only.
- **Screen readers** read the DOM too, with the same limit.
- **A search of yours** over `editor.state` or the document JSON is not affected.
