## Quick start

Zoom through the engine. It needs no markup of yours.

```js
const engine = editor.storage.pageLayout.engine

engine.setZoom(0.75) // three quarters
engine.setZoom('fit') // the largest that fits the width
engine.setZoom(1) // back to full size
```

Which levels to offer and where the control sits is up to you.

## Fit to width

```js
engine.setZoom('fit')
engine.setZoom('fit', 2) // two sheets side by side
```

- **It keeps fitting.** The engine watches the element it is mounted in and fits again when its
  width changes, so a reader dragging the window keeps a page that fits.
- **The widest sheet fits.** A fit is worked out for the widest sheet in the document, not the
  paper it opens with. A landscape section in the middle does not hang off the side.
- **The width is the scroller's.** A fit uses the width of the scroller around the pages, less
  its padding.
- **Never above full size.** A fit scales sheets down, never up.
- **Sheets across.** The second argument is how many sheets fit side by side. It defaults to 1,
  and once set it stays until you pass another.
- **The gap counts.** `setZoom('fit', 2)` leaves room for one `--tt-page-sheet-gap` between the
  two sheets. Widening the gap makes the fit slightly smaller instead of pushing the second
  sheet over the edge.

## Engine methods

These are methods of `editor.storage.pageLayout.engine`, not Commands.

| Method               | Arguments              | Returns     | Description                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `setZoom`            | `level, sheetsAcross?` | `void`      | `level` is a `ZoomLevel`: a factor, or `'fit'`                                 |
| `getZoom`            | —                      | `ZoomLevel` | What you set: a factor, or `'fit'`. `1` before any zoom                        |
| `getScale`           | —                      | `number`    | The factor in force, which is what `'fit'` comes out as                        |
| `getWidestPageWidth` | —                      | `number`    | The width of the widest sheet in CSS pixels. `0` while pageless                |
| `setScale`           | `scale`                | `void`      | Tells the engine the factor a surface that scales the pages itself has applied |

- **Which to ask.** A toolbar showing the selected level asks `getZoom()`. A ruler or an overlay
  that lines up with the pages asks `getScale()`.
- **Scaling the pages yourself.** A surface with a transform of its own calls `setScale` with
  the true factor. Without it, the pages are measured at the scaled size and break in the wrong
  places.

## What a zoom takes care of

- **The pagination does not change.** Every measurement is taken at full size, so a page breaks
  in the same place at 75% as at 100%.
- **The scroll range is what the reader sees.** The engine corrects the height of the element
  it is mounted in, so a document of nine pages does not scroll as though it had twelve.
- **Printing is at full size.** The sheets go to paper at their paper size and come back to the
  reader's zoom afterwards. See [Printing](/page-layout/features/printing).
- **A PDF from a headless browser is at full size.** The scaled element carries
  `data-tt-page-zoom`, and the engine's print rules take the transform off through it. That
  holds without a print event, which a PDF made by a driver does not fire.
- **The factor is in the DOM.** `data-tt-page-zoom` holds the factor, for anything drawn beside
  the pages. It is not set at full size.

## The room around the pages

The desk the sheets lie on is a scroller of yours, and you style it. See
[Styling the pages](/page-layout/guides/styling). `--tt-page-sheet-gap` is the space between two
sheets, in pixels.
